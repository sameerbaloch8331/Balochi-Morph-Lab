About this lab

Balochi is an Iranian language of the Indo-Iranian branch of Indo-European, spoken mainly in Balochistan and neighbouring regions. Morphology is the study of word structure, and the morpheme is the smallest unit of meaning or grammatical function. For example, kitāb + ān gives kitābān “books”.

The lab follows the four ENG 222 tasks: Task 1 morphemes, Task 2 affixes (inflection and derivation), Task 3 word formation, and Task 4 a language showcase. Every section is interactive: tap, flip, sort and answer. The forms follow published descriptions of Modern Standard/Southern Balochi and may vary by dialect.

Task 2 · Affixes

Inflection versus derivation

InflectionalDerivationalWhat separates them
gendit “he/she sees”kanōk “doer”Category and lexeme. gendit is still the same verb. kanōk is a new noun.
gōkay “of the cow”brātōk “little brother”Meaning. The genitive adds a relation only. -ōk changes the meaning.
gendān “I see”nindōk “sitter”Paradigm. gend- takes a full set of endings. -ōk gives one derived form.

Task 2 · Affixes

Exceptions to watch

-ān has two jobs

Plural on nouns (kitābān), 1st person singular on verbs (gendān). The base decides.

-ay has two jobs

2nd person singular (genday) and genitive (gōkay).

-ōk has two jobs

Agent suffix (nindōk) and diminutive (brātōk).

-it or -t?

3rd person singular is -it in gendit but -t in kant.

Is bad- a prefix?

In badkār it is also a free word, so it can be read as a compound.

No infixes found

No infix or circumfix appears in our data, so none is claimed.

Borrowing (Persian)

Not found in our data

Reference

Glossary

MorphemeThe smallest unit of meaning or grammatical function.
Free and bound morphemesA free morpheme can stand alone (āp). A bound one must attach to a base (-ān).
Root, stem, baseThe root is the core lexical unit. The stem takes inflection. The base is any form an affix attaches to.
Prefix and suffixAn affix added before or after a base.
InflectionGrammatical forms of one lexeme: person, number, case.
DerivationBuilding a new lexeme, often with a change of category or meaning.
Compounding and borrowingJoining two stems; taking a word from another language.
AllomorphyDifferent forms of one morpheme, such as -it and -t.
LexicalisationA formed word whose meaning is no longer the plain sum of its parts (delsyāh).
Productivity and wug testHow readily a pattern makes new words. A wug test asks a speaker to judge a new word built on that pattern.
Forms follow published descriptions of Modern Standard/Southern Balochi (Jahani 2019) and the ENG 222 portfolio of Raheel Mairaj and Muhammad Sameer. Balochi varies by dialect, so check forms against the grammar or a speaker. Instructor: Ma’am Muniza Asad.
